What to Measure

"1-in-200 years" is the size of a single loss you'd expect to see about once every 200 years. Op-risk capital models have typically used a one-year 99.9% standard.

Step Through the Threshold

Moving right raises the threshold and leaves fewer, larger losses in the tail. Lower thresholds use more data but bend the fit toward ordinary losses.

    10. Things to Try

    • Press Play and watch the 1-in-200 loss as the threshold rises. It moves from about $49M at the top 50% down to $15M at the top 25%, back up to $31M at the top 10%, and then jumps to $110M with 15 points, where ξ goes above 1 and Expected Shortfall becomes infinite. Same data, one judgment call.
    • Open the loss log, sort by Largest first, and delete the $8,000K unauthorized-trading loss. At the default threshold, ξ falls from 0.51 to 0.27, the 1-in-200 loss from $14.9M to $5.4M, and the 99.9% VaR from $38M to $12M. The lognormal's answer only moves from $3.4M to $2.8M. One event carries most of what the data says about the tail, which is why a single late-discovered loss matters so much.
    • Now put it back with + Add loss at $8,000K, then add a second late-discovered loss of $5,000K, the kind of event the IBNR study shows can surface years later. The 1-in-200 loss rises from $14.9M to about $25M, and the 99.9% VaR from $38M to about $70M.
    • Set the return period to 10 years. The GPD says $3.0M, the lognormal $1.4M, and the normal $1.9M. The gap narrows from 4.4× to about 2×, but the lognormal is still below the two losses over $3.5M the firm actually had in 10 years.
    • Raise the reporting floor to $100K. 112 small losses drop out, leaving 88, and the lognormal's 1-in-200 loss moves from $3.4M to $3.9M because its idea of "typical" changed. Now step to the top 50%, which is 44 points. The GPD gives $16.4M, the same as at 44 points with the full log. The tail fit never used those small losses.
    • Switch the stability chart to Mean excess. For a GPD, the average overshoot above a threshold rises in a straight line, shown here as the gold dashed curve on the log axis. Where the dots start following it is a reasonable place for the threshold.

    Input rules

    • The log needs at least 30 losses at or above the reporting floor, and up to 2,000. Each loss must be above 0.
    • Years of data runs from 1 to 100. Fewer than 5 years gets a soft warning.
    • The return period runs from 2 to 10,000 years, and the confidence level from 90% to 99.99%.
    • The threshold always leaves between 5% and 50% of the losses in the tail, and never fewer than 8 points.
    • If the return period or confidence level asks for a loss smaller than the threshold, the app warns you, because the tail model doesn't cover that range.
